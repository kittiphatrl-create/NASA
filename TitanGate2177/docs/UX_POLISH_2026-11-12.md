# UX / presentation pass — 12 November 2026

Follow-up: [V6 presentation, animation, audio and video evidence](PRESENTATION_POLISH_V6_2026-11-12.md) supersedes the title/status descriptions and build path below.

This pass extends the existing uncommitted Claude implementation. Nothing was reset, committed, pushed or merged. The game remains Unity 6000.3.25f1, built-in renderer and UI Toolkit.

## What changed

- Rebuilt the title screen into a primary-action column and a secondary tools grid. Replaced the Saturn/vortex composition with original procedural Mars art and quieter space layers; shortened development-facing menu labels. Added a readable mission caption panel.
- Replaced narrow vertical resource bottles with compact, labeled reserve cards. Each has a resource icon, percentage, color and fill bar; Tab exposes actual stock units. Percentages are normalized against the largest stock observed in that run, not physical pressure or an invented tank capacity. New runs reset that baseline.
- Added an action-based 06:00–22:00 workday. Walking, reading and conversation do not consume hours. Solar maintenance and drilling cost 3 h, planting 2 h and harvesting 1 h. Resolved event choices cost up to 1 h. Optional work reserves time for the current day's unresolved events. Failed work changes neither time nor stock. The task tooltip shows its duration.
- Evening gathering advances the clock to at least 20:00; sleeping resets the following day to 06:00. Lighting follows the schedule instead of merely counting answered cards. Kept the existing separate visitor/alarm response timers.
- Added morale, explicitly separate from health: starts at 75, pet comfort gives +5 once per day, conversation gives +4 per guest once per day, a fed night gives +3 and hunger gives -15. Values stay within 0–100. It currently communicates wellbeing; there are no undisclosed resource multipliers attached to it.
- Fixed pause gating: the walkable shelter uses chapter 3 internally and was rejected by the old chapter-1/2-only pause check. Fixed the same-frame Escape close/reopen path. Settings now support this chapter and include reduced-motion controls.
- Reduced character world height slightly; made side silhouettes narrower and single-eyed rather than front views sliding sideways. Expanded the stride phases. Added actual front/back pet views in addition to mirrored side views. Kept feet pivots stable.
- Exported directional frame atlases for the cadet, human and alien visitors, dog, cat and rabbit. Fixed the old atlas exporter, whose 22×28 cells cropped the new 32×40 character heads; this was an exporter defect, not the runtime sprite size.
- Added a built-in-compatible additive glow shader for practical lamp pools, retained contact shadows and the existing foreground-wall fading. Added living-area mats and plants in the preceding pass.
- Moved transient notices away from the top-left instruments, limited their width and retained at most two notices.
- Enabled Unity's Advanced Text Generator project setting to match the existing Thai-shaping requests. `TextShapingSetup` uses the actual Unity 6 editor setting and leaves an explicit warning if a later Unity version changes the internal API.

## Research and what was actually viewed

### Video observations

The [official Eastward gameplay preview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hOlZjUSRqiY) was opened and sampled in the browser:

- Around **02:25**: warm storefront lighting, overlapping roof/facade layers and compact characters communicate depth; dialogue is attached to the scene rather than a permanent oversized dashboard.
- Around **07:18**: a small corner HUD leaves most of the playfield available, and silhouettes, foreground props and the darker river separate navigable ground from other layers.

These observations informed hierarchy, scale and negative space. No screenshots, sprites, room layouts, dialogue or audio from that game were imported. This was a targeted visual sampling, not a claim to have watched the complete 15-minute video.

## Practical limits

This is a presentation and interaction pass over the current procedural art, not a complete bespoke redraw of every map or cinematic. No claim that the game is now universally fun, balanced for all new social choices, or accessible on every display. User playtesting is still needed for pacing, repeated dialogue and subjective animation feel. Text scaling beyond the checked defaults and unusual aspect ratios require further validation. The new morale tuning is a starting hypothesis.
