# Titan Gate 2177 — Educator Guide

*Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer · for ages about 10–15 · English and Thai in game*

## The game in one paragraph

It is the year 2177. The player is a cadet astronaut flying the ship *Kestrel* with ORBI, the mission AI,
toward a warp gate at Titan. A piece of space debris hits the ship. In **Chapter 1** the player seals the
breach, reads the fuel gauge and chooses where to go: Mars, home to Earth (it ends on the Moon), or on to
Titan. They then follow that world's real landing procedure and get 60 seconds to stow the supplies they will need (5 of 9 fit). In **Chapter 2** they must survive **7 days**
on that world. Oxygen, water, power and food are linked, and ORBI forecasts each night's danger. Mission
Control asks for science tasks, and some mornings bring a hard choice. The run ends with a **mission
debrief** that awards up to 3 stars and lists what to remember next time.

## What players learn

| Learning goal | Where it happens in the game |
|---|---|
| Life support is a linked system (O₂ ↔ water ↔ power ↔ food) | Every action shows its cost; making oxygen needs power, and splitting water to make oxygen uses up water |
| Each world has its own environment, and that changes how we land | Landing order: heat shield and parachute (Mars, Titan) vs rockets only (Moon) |
| Using local resources (ISRU) | MOXIE oxygen and Sabatier fuel on Mars, ice from the Moon's dark craters, Titan's ice |
| Space radiation and shielding | Rad meter in mSv with real daily values, water as a flare shield, regolith berm |
| Planning under uncertainty | Night forecasts, a big event announced days ahead, real-time emergencies |
| Trade-offs have no single right answer | Morning dilemmas (for example, burn methane for heat but spend oxygen) |
| Real NASA missions and history | 40 knowledge cards; every puzzle answer is written on one |

**No guessing:** every code and every correct answer is printed on a knowledge card. Players learn by
using the facts, not by memorising them for a quiz.

## Running a session

- **Setup:** Windows PC, run `TitanGate2177.exe`. No internet needed. Language can be switched at any time (TH | en).
- **Time:** about 20–30 minutes per route. *This is an estimate; it has not been measured with players yet.*
  Plan one route per lesson; the three routes make a three-lesson unit.
- **Groups:** works well in pairs: one player reads the cards, the other makes the decisions.
- **Accessibility:** text size and dialogue speed in Settings; emergencies give 30 seconds; Esc pauses
  everything, including the timers.
- **If a team loses:** "Retry from landing" restarts Chapter 2 with the same Chapter 1 result.

## The three routes

| Route | Science focus | Signature challenge |
|---|---|---|
| **Mars** (safest) | Thin CO₂ air, MOXIE, Sabatier fuel, dust storms, ancient lakes | Make 100 fuel and launch on Sol 7; survive the great dust storm |
| **The Moon** (medium) | No air, south-pole ice, Earth in the sky, radiation 2× Mars | Repair the dish, aim it at Earth, call the rescue ship |
| **Titan** (hardest) | −179 °C, thick nitrogen air, methane rain, RTG power | Unlock and charge the warp gate; survive Saturn's shadow |

| Group | Cards |

| Deep space & living in space | Titan · Space debris (~7 km/s) · Whipple shield (ISS) · Fuel and delta-v · Radiation dose (mSv; Curiosity RAD, Chang'e 4, NASA 600 mSv career limit) · Veggie (ISS lettuce, 2015) |
| Mars | 7 minutes of terror (Perseverance EDL) · The sol (24 h 39 m 35 s) · Mars air (95% CO₂) · MOXIE (2021–2023) · Water recycling (ISS ~98%) · Ice under Mars (Phoenix 2008) · Solar power and dust (InSight) · Sabatier reaction (nickel catalyst) · Space radiation and water · Dust storms (2018, Opportunity) · Blue sunsets (Curiosity 2015) · Mars once had lakes (Jezero) · Thin Martian air (<1% pressure) |
| The Moon | Landing with no air (Apollo LM) · Apollo 11 (1969, 3 days) · Ice in dark craters (LCROSS 2009) · The south pole · Sharp moon dust · Earth in the Moon's sky · Electrolysis (ISS oxygen) · Regolith shielding · Earthrise (Apollo 8, 1968) · Ice is rocket fuel (Artemis) · Moonquakes (Apollo seismometers) |
| Titan | Gravity assist (Voyager, Cassini) · Huygens landing (2005) · −179 °C · Thick nitrogen air · RTG power · Methane lakes (Kraken Mare) · Dragonfly (planned 2028 launch) · Orange haze · Ocean under the ice (Cassini) · Organic dunes |

Cards found are kept in the in-game card book between runs, so a class can track how many they have found.

## Reading the debrief

| Star | Earned by | Talk about |
|---|---|---|
| ★ Mission complete | Surviving 7 days and reaching the goal | Which resource was the bottleneck? |
| ★ Healthy | Radiation ≤ 50 mSv and never collapsing from exhaustion | Why did water at bedtime matter during a flare? |
| ★ Science | All 3 Mission Control tasks | Was the science worth the time it cost? |

The "What to remember next time" list explains every missed night or emergency in one science sentence.
It works well as an exit ticket: ask each pair to copy one line and explain it in their own words.

## Discussion questions

**Before playing**
1. What does a person need to stay alive for a week? Which of those can you make on another world?
2. Why might landing on Mars be different from landing on the Moon?

**During play** (pause with Esc)
3. ORBI says tonight brings a solar flare. What will you do with your actions today, and why?
4. Your oxygen generator needs power. Where does your power come from on this world?

**After playing**
5. Compare your route with a team that played another route. Which world was harder to live on, and why?
6. Which morning choice was hardest? What did each option cost?
7. NASA limits an astronaut's career dose to 600 mSv. At the Moon's ~1.4 mSv per day, how many days on the
   surface is that? *(about 430 days)* What could make a base safer?

## Extension activities

- **Dose math:** using the Radiation dose card, compare one week on the ISS, Mars, the Moon and Titan.
- **Delta-v budget:** draw the three destinations as costs. Why can a low-fuel ship still reach Titan in the
  game? (It uses a gravity assist at Jupiter. See the Gravity assist card.)
- **Design a habitat:** sketch a Moon base that uses regolith, ice and sunlight from the south pole.
- **Real missions:** each team picks one card and finds the real NASA mission page to present for one minute.

## Notes for accuracy

The game simplifies on purpose: resources are 0–100 bars, the days are short, and the warp gate is
fiction. The facts on the knowledge cards are real and come from NASA missions. Daily radiation values
use published measurements (Curiosity RAD on Mars, Chang'e 4 LND on the Moon) and are rounded.
