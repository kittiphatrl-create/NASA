# Titan Gate 2177 — Educator Guide

*Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer · for ages about 7–12 (older players welcome) · English and Thai in game*

## The game in one paragraph

It is the year 2177. The player is a cadet astronaut on the ship *Kestrel* with ORBI, the mission AI. Space debris
hits the ship. In **Chapter 1** the player seals the breach, sees that the fuel left only reaches Mars, follows the
real Mars landing procedure, and has **60 seconds** to stow 5 of 10 loose supplies before touchdown. In
**Chapter 2** the landed Kestrel becomes a **Mars outpost**. The player keeps it alive for **7 days (168 hours)**
until a rescue ship lands: every job costs hours, power must be split between systems, a **solar storm** arrives
on day 4, and messages with Earth take time. The run ends with a **mission debrief** of up to 3 stars.

## The trade-offs players feel

| Trade-off | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Power budget** | Base Control: life support, greenhouse lamps, rescue beacon and shelter support all draw power every hour. Solar panels only charge 07:00–19:00, so you can't run everything, and night power must be saved. |
| **Air vs. power** | Life support and MOXIE make air but use power. Switch life support off and the air drops fast. |
| **Radiation shielding** | Earth warns of a solar storm. Building the shelter (water wall, regolith sandbags, radiation blanket) costs supplies, energy or hours; powering it during the storm costs power. |
| **Food production** | The greenhouse needs a setup job plus 24 hours of light; it competes with everything else for power. |
| **Communication delay** | Earth messages ride a relay orbiter (10:00 and 22:00) plus a 14-minute light delay. Ask Earth and wait, or read the knowledge cards and act now. |
| **Time** | 168 hours sounds like a lot until every repair, walk and meal takes 1–4 of them. |

No choice is free, and no stowed supply is required to win: supplies only make some jobs faster or cheaper.

## Real NASA science vs. game numbers

The in-game **Science & Data Sources** screen separates three kinds of content:
1. **Observed by NASA** — Mars surface radiation about 0.64 mSv/day (Curiosity RAD, Hassler et al. 2014; the game rounds to 0.7); the September 2017 solar storm seen at Mars by Curiosity RAD and MAVEN; MOXIE making oxygen on Perseverance (2021–2023); the length of a sol.
2. **NASA science principles** — DONKI space weather alerts; ECLSS life support; plant growth in space; water and regolith as radiation shields; the 4–24 minute Earth–Mars radio delay.
3. **Game simulation** — the 7-day rescue, the storm's size and timing, every penalty, and the 0–100 bars.

## Running a session

- **Setup:** Windows PC, run `TitanGate2177.exe` (a browser build is planned). No account, no internet.
- **Time:** one run is roughly 20–30 minutes. *Estimate, not yet measured with players.*
- **Groups:** works well in pairs: one reads the cards and the forecast, the other makes the decisions.
- **Accessibility:** text size and dialogue speed in Settings; Esc pauses everything; emergencies give 30 seconds; a low-air alarm wakes the astronaut instead of failing silently.
- **If a team loses:** "Retry from landing" restarts Chapter 2 with the same stowed supplies.

## Reading the debrief

| Star | Earned by | Talk about |
|---|---|---|
| ★ Rescued | Coordinates acknowledged and alive at hour 168 | When did you send the coordinates? Why early? |
| ★ Healthy | Radiation ≤ 50 mSv and never collapsed | How ready was the shelter when the storm hit? |
| ★ Tasks | All 3 science tasks and a ready greenhouse | What did you give up to do science? |

Three endings: **Rescued**, **Survived but Stranded** (alive, no coordinates), **Simulation Failed** (air or supplies ran out) — each explains what to change.

## Discussion questions

1. Before playing: what would you need to stay alive on Mars for a week? Which of those can you make there?
2. During play (Esc pauses): it's 18:00 and the battery is half full. Which systems will you leave on tonight, and why?
3. Earth says a storm arrives on day 4. What will you build first, and what does it cost?
4. Why can't Mission Control simply tell you what to do the moment something breaks?
5. After playing: NASA limits an astronaut's career dose to 600 mSv. At about 0.64 mSv/day on Mars' surface, how many days is that? *(about 940 days)*

## Knowledge cards (25)

Deep space: Titan, space debris, Whipple shield, delta-v, radiation dose, Veggie (ISS lettuce), far from home (Mars-500). Mars: landing (EDL), the sol, Mars air, MOXIE, water recycling, ice, solar power and dust, Sabatier reaction, space radiation and water, dust storms, blue sunsets, ancient lakes, thin air, regolith shielding, CO₂ frost, the September 2017 solar storm, life support (ECLSS), talking to Earth takes time.

## Notes for accuracy

The game simplifies on purpose. Facts on the cards come from NASA missions; numbers that are game balance are labelled as simulation in the Sources screen and never presented as live data.
